Floor 2 · Understand · Member

Who really does it?

I cut my hair. → I did it (with scissors, in my bathroom).
I had my hair cut. → a stylist did it. I was the VIP.

The subject doesn't do the action: the subject causes it. That's the whole idea. The rest is choosing how much power you use.

En español: "Me corté el pelo" es ambiguo. En inglés debes elegir: cut (yo) o had cut (otro).

Power meter: how bossy are you?

VerbUse it when…Example
haveservices or delegating with normal authorityI had the mechanic check the brakes.
getconvincing, or informal talkI got my dad to lend me the car.
makeforcing, or causing a reactionThe movie made me cry.
letgiving permissionMom let me go out.
helpassisting (to optional)She helped me (to) finish.

Tenses: where does it live?

Only have / get / make / let change tense. The participle or base verb never changes.

Very common: past simple (I had my hair cut yesterday), will / going to (I'll have it sent), present simple for habits (I have my car washed every Saturday), present continuous (I'm having my house painted), modals (You should have your eyes checked).
Rare: present perfect continuous, past perfect, future perfect. They are correct, but you'll almost never hear them.

Ojo: have y get causativos no tienen pasiva. Make sí: be made to + verbo.

Floor 4 · Analyze · Executive

Error Hunters

Each sentence was "written by a VIP" and has a mistake. Find why it's wrong.

Patterns to compare

had the mechanic check ✔ / had the mechanic checked ✘
got the mechanic to check ✔ / got the mechanic check ✘
I had my car repaired = someone repaired it. I had repaired my car = past perfect, I did it.
Questions: Did you have your car repaired? ✔ / Have you your car repaired? ✘
Careful: He had his wallet stolen is bad luck, not a request. Context decides.

Part 2 · Real conversations

The Conversation Room

Now the VIPs talk to you. Knowing the structure is not enough: you must understand what the other person means, respond well, and notice when someone goes too far. Same six Bloom steps, in real dialogue.

Aquí no solo dices el causativo: lo escuchas, lo interpretas, respondes y juzgas si fue excesivo.

1 · Remember: spot the verb

2 · Understand: what are they telling you?

3 · Apply: how do you answer?

4 · Analyze: same words, different pressure

5 · Evaluate: appropriate, excessive or too soft?

6 · Create: write the conversation

Write 6 lines. Include: one request that is appropriate, one that goes too far, and a polite response from the other person (Would you mind…? / I’d rather… / Could you let me…?).

Part 3 · Practice until it is automatic

The Construction Gym

Five stages. First you see the model in every tense. Then you build sentences with labeled words, then with words alone. At the end you write them yourself for the occasion.

Stage 1 · Model: every tense, every occasion

Los tiempos compuestos (perfect continuous, future perfect) existen, pero casi no se usan con el causativo.

Stage 2 · Labeled words: build with support

Each word shows its role. Tap them in order.

Stage 3 · Words alone: build without help

No labels now. Use the tense and occasion to decide the order.

Stage 4 · Write it for the occasion

Now nobody gives you the words. Read the situation and write the sentence.

Stage 5 · The Occasion Machine

You only get a situation. You choose the verb and the structure.
